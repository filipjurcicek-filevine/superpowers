## Regression to the mean

### Do not inflate significance

**Words to watch:** *stands/serves as*, *is a testament/reminder*, *plays a
vital/significant/crucial/pivotal role*, *underscores/highlights its
importance/significance*, *reflects broader*, *symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting
impact*, *key turning point*, *indelible mark*, *deeply rooted*, *profound heritage*,
*steadfast dedication*

Do not tell the reader that something matters; state what it does and let the fact
carry the weight.

### Do not certify importance by citation

**Words to watch:** *independent coverage*, *local/regional/national/[country name]
media outlets*, *music/business/tech outlets*, *active social media presence*

Do not argue that a subject is important by listing who covered it; cite a source for
the claim it supports and keep the coverage out of the prose.

### Do not analyze without saying anything

**Words to watch:** *ensuring ...*, *highlighting ...*, *emphasizing ...*, *reflecting
...*, *underscoring ...*, *showcasing ...*, *aligns with...*, *contributing to...*

Do not end a sentence with a participial phrase that tells the reader what the fact
means; a fact cannot highlight, underscore, or reflect anything.

### Do not sell

**Words to watch:** *continues to captivate*, *groundbreaking* (in the figurative
sense), *stunning natural beauty*, *enduring/lasting legacy*, *nestled*, *in the heart
of*, *boasts a*

Do not write in the register of a brochure; say what a thing is and does, not how
impressive it is.

### Do not lecture the reader

**Words to watch:** *it's important/critical/crucial to note/remember/consider*, *may
vary*

Do not tell the reader what is important to note or remember; if it matters, state it
and drop the frame.

### Do not append a summary that adds nothing

**Words to watch:** *In summary*, *In conclusion*, *Overall*

Do not close with a sentence or section that restates what the reader just read.

### Do not close with challenges and future prospects

**Words to watch:** *Despite its... faces several challenges...*, *Despite these
challenges*, *Challenges and Legacy*, *Future Outlook*

Do not end on the formula of conceding challenges and then speculating about prospects;
name a specific open problem or stop.

## Language and grammar

### Do not use AI vocabulary

**Words to watch:** *align/aligns/aligning with*, *crucial*, *delve/delves/delving*
(pre-2025), *emphasizing*, *enduring*, *enhance/enhances/enhancing*, *fostering*,
*garnered/garnering*, *highlight/highlighted/highlighting/highlights* (as a verb),
*interplay*, *intricate/intricacies*, *key* (as an adjective), *landscape*,
*leveraging*, *multifaceted*, *notably*, *nuanced*, *realm*, *robust*,
*seamless/seamlessly*, *shed light on*, *showcasing*, *streamline*, *tapestry*,
*testament*, *underpin/underpins/underpinning*, *underscore/underscores/underscoring*,
*vibrant*, *vital*

They arrive in clusters, and the cluster is the tell; a single apt use is not. Where
one of them stands in for a plainer and more specific word, write the plainer word.

### Do not write "not just X, but Y"

Do not build a sentence on the negation of a smaller claim — "it is not just a
refactor, it is a rethink" — because the negated half is filler.

### Do not define a thing by what it is not

Do not stack absences to build to a point; say what is there and let the gap speak for
itself.

### Do not force triples

Do not pad a list to three items for rhythm; give the items that exist.

### Do not attribute opinions vaguely

**Words to watch:** *Industry reports*, *Observers have cited*, *Some critics argue*

Do not credit a claim to an unnamed authority; name who said it, or cut the claim.

### Do not vary a term for variety's sake

Do not substitute synonyms for something you have already named; use the same term
every time.

### Do not write false ranges

Do not write "from X to Y" unless X and Y are the two ends of one real scale that has a
middle.

### Do not use title case in headings

Do not capitalize every main word in a heading; capitalize the first word and proper
nouns only.

## Punctuation and formatting

The rules on title case, bold, and inline-header bullet lists govern prose
documents; skill files in this library use bold labels and inline headers as
navigation deliberately.

### Do not bold for emphasis

Do not bold a phrase to make it land; bold only a term the reader has to be able to
find again.

### Do not write inline-header bullet lists

Do not open every bullet with a bold label and a colon; write the bullet as a sentence.

### Do not decorate with emoji

Do not put emoji in prose, including in front of a heading or a bullet.

## Communication intended for the user

### Do not hedge about your knowledge cutoff

**Words to watch:** *as of [date]*, *Up to my last training update*, *as of my last
knowledge update*, *While specific details are limited/scarce...*, *not widely
available/documented/disclosed*, *...in the provided/available sources/search
results...*, *based on available information*

Do not warn the reader that your information may be dated or your sources thin, and
never speculate about what the missing material probably said.

### Do not leak refusal language

**Words to watch:** *as an AI language model*, *as a large language model*, *I'm sorry*

Do not leave an apology or a reference to yourself as a language model in the text you
deliver.

### Do not ship placeholder text

Do not deliver text that still carries unfilled brackets, placeholder dates, or "add if
available" comments.
